How the points work

Each entry is scored in seven areas of Black American life: Image (how Black Americans were seen), Wealth, Education, Power (political and institutional), Rights & Safety, Health and Culture. In each area, the single best event on the timeline is set at +5 and the single worst at −5. Every other event is placed between them in quarter-point steps (+4.75, +4.5 … +0.25) by comparing it with those two extremes. Areas an event didn't touch score 0. Firsts (the first Black American to hold an office, win a title or reach a milestone) count +0.25 in each area they touched. Power gains top out at +3, because Black Americans have won community power but not lasting national or state power; Power losses still reach −5.

One event can help some areas and hurt others, so an entry is labeled Gain, Setback or Mixed. Chains of cause and effect are listed as “Led to” and “Followed from”; each event keeps only its own direct points, so nothing is counted twice along a chain.

In By theme, an entry in two themes counts its total in both lines, so theme lines are best compared with each other rather than added together. The graph charts the documented record on this timeline, not a measured statistic like income or enrollment.
